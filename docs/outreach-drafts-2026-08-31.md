## NUS research-interest statement — working draft

Target length: no more than 500 words when attached to a first-contact email.

### Measuring coordination delay in tourist medical emergencies in Thailand

Tourist medical emergencies in Thailand often cross institutional boundaries:
a remote clinic or emergency service identifies a need for higher-level care,
an assistance operator coordinates transport and payment, and a receiving
facility accepts the patient. Each organization may perform its part, yet no
single record shows where coordination time was lost.

I operate Tourist SOS, a Thailand-based coordination service, and have developed
a de-identified research programme around this gap. A historical registry of
836 cases from December 2018 to March 2020 supports descriptive analysis of
case mix, payer fragmentation, and evacuation patterns. Its most important
finding is negative: the operational records preserve what happened but not the
timestamps needed to calculate time to transport activation, guaranteed payment,
or definitive care. No historical case supports an end-to-end coordination
interval.

The proposed doctorate would evaluate a prospective measurement and
human-in-the-loop coordination intervention rather than attempt to reconstruct
missing historical time. The first study would report the historical baseline
and the limits of routinely collected operational data. A prospective
feasibility study would then test whether milestone timestamps, recording lag,
operator burden, and human decisions on AI-generated recommendations can be
captured reliably in live operations. A later multi-corridor study would proceed
only if enrolment volume and measurement fidelity support it.

The programme is deliberately not an autonomous clinical-AI project. The system
coordinates rather than diagnoses; a human operator accepts or overrides every
recommendation. The research questions concern measurement validity, health-
system coordination, implementation, and the conditions under which decision
support helps or harms workflow.

NUS is relevant because the work requires both health-systems supervision and
strong quantitative/AI methods. The project also raises governance questions
that need institutional treatment: retrospective use of de-identified company
records, prospective emergency data, conflict-of-interest management, Thai PDPA,
cross-border data access, and preservation of the operational data source while
the research is conducted.

I am seeking an advisor willing to help reshape this operationally grounded
programme into a defensible public-health doctorate, including narrowing the
primary question, selecting the appropriate feasibility design, and determining
whether the continuing Thailand operation is compatible with NUS candidature.

## Laerdal four-page concept skeleton

Prepare only after applicant eligibility is cleared.

### 1. Problem and significance

- Time-critical tourist trauma crosses clinic, assistance, payer, transport, and
  referral boundaries.
- The 836-case historical registry cannot measure coordination delay because the
  necessary milestone timestamps were not captured.
- The proposed work is a prospective measurement-feasibility study, not a
  commercial product-development request or an efficacy trial.

### 2. Aim

Determine whether a human-in-the-loop coordination protocol can capture
protocol-valid milestone times, recording lag, operator burden, and decision
provenance in the first 20 eligible and consented cases.

### 3. Methods

- one ethics-approved Thailand corridor;
- consecutive screening and enrolment ledger;
- predefined milestones and time-resolution rules;
- human acceptance or override of every decision-support recommendation;
- outcomes: milestone completeness, interval completeness, recording lag,
  operator burden, decision completion, adherence, and safety events;
- descriptive feasibility analysis only.

### 4. Expected value

- establishes whether an hours-scale coordination study is technically and
  operationally possible;
- identifies where missingness and recording burden arise;
- produces a defensible go/no-go decision for broader evaluation;
- avoids claiming that decision support reduces delay before evidence exists.

### 5. Budget headings

- research coordination and data-quality review;
- ethics and governance support;
- statistical/methodological support;
- operator training and protocol materials;
- secure research data handling;
- dissemination of the feasibility result.

Exclude product engineering, general company operations, equipment-only spending,
construction, marketing, and conference/travel-led costs.
